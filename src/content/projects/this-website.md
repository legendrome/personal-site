## The problem

I wanted a home on the internet that reads like a person, not a CV, without paying for a website builder. I had no web development background. So I set myself one rule: whatever we built, I had to be able to change it myself, without AI.

## What I built

A small site made with Astro and plain HTML and CSS, hosted for free on Cloudflare. Every word lives in a plain text file, and every colour and font sits in one place, so changing a sentence never means touching code. There is a guide in the repo that walks through adding a post, a chapter or a project, step by step.

The design is a dive. You start on a boat above the waves, and each chapter of my life sits a little deeper, all the way down to the sea floor.

I built it with AI over about a week, one piece at a time, looking at a mock of every page before it was built.

## What broke

The first designs didn't feel like me. Poster layouts, loud fonts and a deep space colour scheme came together quickly, and I couldn't connect with any of them. The ocean dive came the day after, and it stuck.

The first time I added a post on my own, the build refused it. I had copied the example from the guide along with the markers around it, and the error named the file but not the cause. The guide got a new section on exactly that, and my next try went live with no help.

A sanity check later found too many ocean puns, three pages that all opened the same way, and a starfish that looked like clip art. Those are fixed now, and the starfish is a basketball.

## What changed

AI made things fast, but making them mine was still my job. The more clearly I knew what I wanted, the better it got, which is what I wrote about in [my first post](/writing/creating-a-website-with-ai).

Every lesson went into the guide or a decisions file, so the next person, or me in a year, can pick it up without asking anyone.
