---
title: "Hyderabad"
place: "Hyderabad"
line: "Where I learned the value of culture and friendships."
start: 2016
end: 2023
---

In 2016 we moved to Hyderabad, for family reasons. That wasn't my choice either. It simply was the next stage.

We had visited India almost every year since moving to Sweden, so India was not exactly new to me. But trying to coexist with the people definitely was. Hyderabad was the catalyst for who I am today.

I arrived as a non-resident Indian who had spent most of his life in Sweden, and I felt like an imposter. Most people in Hyderabad speak Telugu or Hindi. Thankfully, my parents had made sure I spoke Telugu, but I didn't speak any Hindi, and it was difficult to pick up after spending most of my life learning Swedish. My school was international and most people did speak English, but a lot of the casual banter happened in Hindi, and I felt very left out.

For the first few years I had a small group of friends I could count on one hand, and I didn't go out much. I still did a lot of extracurriculars, mostly basketball and badminton, because there was a court near home. I built a small but tight-knit community of friends I could rely on, a few at the apartments and a few at school. From August to December 2019 I was away in Kenya, which has its own chapter. I thought life was good. I thought I was enjoying it, up until 2020, when Covid hit.

I could never forget those two years. At first I thought being stuck at home was a blessing. School moved online and felt inconsequential, and I spent most of my time doing whatever I wanted. I played a lot of online games with my friends, so my social life wasn't zero.

But almost a year in, I realised I was yearning to know more people. I loved my friends as they were, but I didn't know much about my classmates. I had spent a lot of time with them at school, but we were only ever friends by happenstance, and I didn't like that. I wanted to be friends with them because I made the effort to. That feeling of wanting to create bonds with the people around me is what changed the most in Hyderabad.

So when the Covid restrictions were lifting in 2021, I joined my school's residential programme, and I never looked back. My two IB years were the best time of my life. I got to the dorms and immediately found a group of people, and for the first six months I spent a lot of time around the people I really wanted to get closer to.

Then came the student council election, towards the end of the first year, with my best friend at the time and me as rivals. I went to a group of guys in my class I hadn't really spoken to much. They had a strong sense of right and wrong, conviction and togetherness. They had been refused the chance to take part in the elections because of the academic requirements, so getting their help was a hard sell. I spoke to them and explained why I wanted to be president and the ideas I had, and they seemed to agree.

As I got closer to them, I quickly felt a real sense of kinship. And I won. That group of nine or ten boys are still my friends today, and we still speak every other week.

Those two years, dealing with all kinds of people and working from a place of responsibility as student council president, taught me a lot about who I was, the kind of people I enjoy being around, and the kind of work I enjoy doing. I realised I enjoy hands-on work, where the impact is tangible and I can see it.

What I took with me when I left in 2023 was the ability to enter a new environment, live by myself, learn and perform.
