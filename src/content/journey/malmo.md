I moved to Malmö in 2006, when I was very young. I did not choose to be there. It just happened, for family reasons, and to this day I think it's the best decision my family has ever taken.

The memory that comes back first is a parent-teacher meeting with my grade 4 teacher. She was a lovely lady, always cheerful. But she sat me down and told me I was a very smart child who liked to have fun, and that I needed to decide whether or not I wanted to learn these subjects. It felt like I wasn't always paying attention in class, she said, and maybe that was because it was too easy for me and I needed more of a challenge. If I wanted a challenge, she would try to help me. If I just didn't want to learn, there was nothing she could do for me.

That stuck with me for a very long time. Up until then, my parents were the reason I was trying so hard to achieve a lot academically. It was not an overnight change, but slowly, over time, I started paying more attention in class, taking on more assignments from her, and trying different things by myself and with my best friend. Just exploring.

My school was very multicultural. I got to meet people from India, like me, and from Japan, China, Greece, Turkey, America and, obviously, Sweden. Being around that much culture at that young an age, I never thought to look for differences between people. It never occurred to me that we should treat people differently based on where they're from or how they speak. I guess that was the first time I was learning to be open-minded.

I learned a good bit about myself too. I loved spending time with my friends, but I also enjoyed my own time, reading my books and playing my games. I was naturally curious, with a short attention span. And whatever extracurricular I took up, I always wanted to get better at it.

There were a lot of them: swimming, cricket, the violin, which I later dropped for the guitar, the choir and the school plays. And badminton, how could I forget badminton? It was my and my best friend's favourite sport. We played it so much that we joined a club, and we ended up competing in under 12 and under 13 tournaments, including against Danish clubs.

I never thought about taking anything specific with me when I left. But in hindsight, spending so much time in a very open, progressive and diverse society showed me, from a very young age, the many kinds of people there are in the world. I think it made me a lot more perceptive about cultures, feelings, ideas and expressions, and that has helped my social skills throughout my life.
